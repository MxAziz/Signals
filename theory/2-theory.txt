Theory Notes — Experiments 7–13

-----------------------------------
EXPERIMENT 07: Spectrum of a Signal
-----------------------------------
Experiment Name:
Finding the spectrum of a composite sinusoidal signal.

Objective:
To obtain and plot the frequency spectrum of the given signal.

Theory:
The spectrum shows the frequency components present in a signal.
Fourier transform or FFT converts a time-domain signal into a frequency-domain representation.
The magnitude spectrum indicates the strength of each frequency component.

-----------------------------------
EXPERIMENT 08: Removing Noise from Audio Signal
-----------------------------------
Experiment Name:
Noise removal from a signal using frequency-domain filtering.

Objective:
To generate a 440 Hz signal, add noise, analyze it using DFT, remove high-frequency noise and reconstruct the signal.

Theory:
The DFT represents a noisy signal in the frequency domain.
A high-frequency filter suppresses unwanted components above a selected cutoff frequency.
The IDFT converts the filtered spectrum back to the time domain.

-----------------------------------
EXPERIMENT 09: PPG Signal Analysis
-----------------------------------
Experiment Name:
Extraction of systolic peaks, diastolic points and heart rate from a PPG signal.

Objective:
To analyze a PPG signal and determine important cardiac features and heart rate.

Theory:
Photoplethysmography (PPG) measures blood-volume changes.
Systolic peaks are prominent pulses in the PPG waveform.
Diastolic points occur near the lower portions between successive pulses.
Heart rate can be estimated from the time interval between consecutive systolic peaks.

-----------------------------------
EXPERIMENT 10: Power Density Spectrum
-----------------------------------
Experiment Name:
Power Density Spectrum of a square wave.

Objective:
To generate a square wave and observe its power distribution in the frequency domain.

Theory:
The power spectrum describes how signal power is distributed over frequency.
A periodic square wave contains power mainly at its fundamental frequency and odd harmonics.
FFT can be used to obtain the frequency-domain representation and calculate power.

-----------------------------------
EXPERIMENT 11: Fourier Series Approximation
-----------------------------------
Experiment Name:
Fourier series approximation of a square wave.

Objective:
To approximate a square wave using a finite number of Fourier series terms.

Theory:
A square wave can be represented as:
x(t) = (4/π)[sin(ω0t) + (1/3)sin(3ω0t) + (1/5)sin(5ω0t) + ...].
Only odd harmonics are present in this series.
Adding more harmonics improves the approximation.

-----------------------------------
EXPERIMENT 12: Amplitude Spectrum
-----------------------------------
Experiment Name:
Amplitude spectrum of a sum of cosine signals and numerical Fourier transform.

Objective:
To find the amplitude spectrum and approximate the Fourier transform over 0–900 Hz.

Theory:
A cosine signal produces frequency components at its positive and negative frequencies.
For the given sum of cosines, the amplitude spectrum contains components at 100, 500 and 700 Hz.
The Fourier transform can be approximated numerically by evaluating the transform integral over sampled time values.

-----------------------------------
EXPERIMENT 13: DTFT
-----------------------------------
Experiment Name:
Determination of the DTFT of x[n] = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}.

Objective:
To calculate and plot the magnitude, angle, real and imaginary parts of the DTFT.

Theory:
The DTFT is:
X(e^jω) = Σ x[n]e^(−jωn).
It gives a continuous-frequency representation of a discrete-time sequence.
The DTFT is generally complex and can be represented by magnitude, phase, real and imaginary parts.
In this experiment, 501 equally spaced frequency points are used from 0 to π.
